The square numbers (1 to 32) are the ones used on the score sheet, the way checkers games have been written down for two hundred years.

How to Play

Each side starts with twelve pieces on the dark squares. Black moves first, then the players take turns. You win by capturing all of your opponent's pieces, or by leaving them with no move to make.

  1. Moving. Pieces move one square diagonally forward onto an empty dark square. Click a piece and then a square, or drag it there. Dots show where it can go.
  2. Jumping. Capture an enemy piece by jumping diagonally over it onto the empty square just beyond. If you can keep jumping from where you land, you must, even changing direction. Click each landing square in turn, or just the last one.
  3. You must jump. When a capture is available you have to take one (pick whichever you like). Pieces that can jump glow gold. In the options on the title screen you can make jumping optional, as some families do.
  4. Kings. A piece that reaches the far row is crowned and becomes a king, and its move ends there. Kings move and jump diagonally both forward and backward.
  5. Draws. The same position three times, forty moves each with no capture and no plain piece moved, or a draw offer that's accepted.

Six regulars at the Hollow Creek General Store will give you a game, from Little Tucker (just learning) to Old Gus, county champion. Your record against each of them is kept, and an unfinished game is waiting when you come back.
